Stronger together • A practical field guide

Build strength.
Find your pace.
Keep showing up.

Two shared gym days. Two individual paths.
More energy for the court, the classroom, work and family life.

Youth track
Tennis + stamina

For a growing teen beginning structured fitness. Build comfortable running, movement skills and confidence.

Adult track
Strength + health

For a midlife gym beginner who already runs 5 km. Build consistent strength while maintaining aerobic fitness.

Start with a 12-week habit block. The running stages may take longer. Success means recovering well and wanting to return, with steadier tennis participation and easier everyday movement.

Before the first hard session
Because the youth is struggling to complete tennis, arrange a pediatric review before adding sprints or pushing endurance, especially if this is new or worsening. Do not assume body size explains fatigue. Tell the clinician about wheeze, cough, dizziness, chest symptoms, sleep and recent illness. Easy, symptom-free activity can be the starting point while seeking advice.

This is a suggested starting framework, not an individual medical assessment. Review it with the tennis coach and a qualified trainer experienced with adolescents. REP is the app's illustrated guide, not a credentialed human trainer. No names, exact ages, body measurements or personal health labels are included.

Make the youth load fit

This assumes one tennis session per week. With two tennis days, replace a run with tennis. With matches or a camp, remove the speed add-on and reduce gym sets. Ask the coach to coordinate the whole week, including demanding PE.

Keep 1-2 days free of organized sport. Avoid consecutive hard leg days. If tennis falls after a run, keep the run easy and short, or walk.

Build daily movement gradually

The longer-term youth guideline is 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity daily, including school, play and sport. Vigorous, muscle- and bone-strengthening activity belong on at least 3 days/week. This is a direction to build toward, not extra workouts to force into week one. [1]

Adult aerobic target: at least 150 moderate minutes/week or 75 vigorous minutes, plus strength on 2 days. [1]

REP's coaching cue

“Train together, choose your own load. A shared appointment matters more than matching speed, weight or repetitions.”

First fortnight: use one easy set per youth exercise, two per adult exercise; start running conservatively. Book a gym orientation and check youth access rules, supervision requirements and whether machines fit.

How to progress a youth exercise

First improve control, then repetitions, then resistance. After two comfortable visits at the top of the range, a trainer may select the smallest suitable load increase and return to the lower end. If the next machine plate is a big jump, stay at the same load. Increase only one variable at a time.

REP's coaching cue

“Smooth repetitions are the achievement. End the set when technique changes, even if the screen still shows a target.”

A parent training nearby is helpful but does not replace initial instruction from someone skilled in youth resistance training. Leave at least 48 hours between the two gym sessions. Do not perform adult loads as a family challenge. [2]

REP.04 / Youth running progression

From run/walk to a comfortable 5K.

Use time and the talk test. There is no deadline and no required pace.

Entry point: first be comfortable walking briskly for 20-30 minutes without symptoms or next-day pain. If not, begin with 10-15 min walks and gradually extend them. Follow any clinician advice from page 1.

Every run: walk 5 min before and after. Jog slowly enough to speak a full sentence, about 3-4 out of 10 effort. Walk sooner if that changes. The table is the main block only; take the walk recovery after every jog, including the last.

Move on only after two comfortable sessions at the same stage with normal walking, stairs and energy the next day. Start with two runs/week separated by a non-running day. A stage may take several weeks; 12-24+ weeks is fine. Insert shorter intervals between stages if needed. Never advance running and add speed work in the same week.

The distance bridge: 30 minutes may be less than 5 km. After stage 11 feels routine, add only 3-5 minutes to one easy outing in a successful week, using walk breaks, until 5 km fits comfortably. Keep the other run at 20-30 minutes. Stop at the planned time even if the distance is unfinished.

How this relates to Couch to 5K
This is a custom, slower two-run framework inspired by run/walk training, not the official NHS plan or a validated pediatric protocol. The NHS version uses three runs/week across nine stages/weeks, ending at 30 minutes. If choosing that version with the coach, follow it instead of this table and reduce other training to preserve recovery. [3]

REP.05 / Court fitness + injury decisions

Build the engine.
Then add a little speed.

Easy running supports endurance. Short, well-rested accelerations practice tennis movement; exhausting sprints are not the starting point.

First 2-4 weeks: finish tennis with something left

Ask the coach to shorten drills and agree on planned water/rest breaks before fatigue escalates. A starting example is 5-8 min of comfortable skill work followed by 1-2 min recovery, repeated within a shortened lesson. The coach should adjust this to the player's current tolerance and keep technique central.

Only when ready: a small speed practice

After at least 3-4 comfortable weeks, and only with normal recovery, no pain or exercise symptoms and the coach's agreement, add this once weekly inside tennis. It replaces part of the lesson's conditioning; it is not an extra training day.

  1. Warm up 10-12 min: easy movement, marching, side steps and 2 gentle build-ups.
  2. Do 4 × 5-second controlled accelerations, around 70-80% of perceived top speed, on a dry, flat, clear surface. Walk 60-90 sec between efforts.
  3. Leave plenty of space to slow down gradually. No maximal racing, sharp shuttle turns or treadmill sprints.
  4. After two comfortable weekly practices, the coach may choose 5-6 efforts. Increase repetitions or speed, never both together. Skip during heavy tennis weeks.

The exact acceleration dose is a conservative coaching suggestion, not a medical threshold. It is optional: enjoyable tennis and easy aerobic work can remain the full plan.

What you noticeWhat to do
Comfortable effortBreathing settles with rest, movement stays smooth, and next-day energy is normal: repeat or progress one small step.
Recovery is falling behindHeavy legs, mild muscle soreness, poor sleep or declining enthusiasm: remove speed work; cut the next workout roughly in half or take a rest day. Resume progression only when recovered.
Pain or recurring symptomsStop for sharp/localized pain, swelling, limping, wheeze, dizziness or unusual breathlessness. Seek medical advice before resuming the provoking activity; persistent or recurrent knee, heel, shin or back pain needs assessment.
Emergency signsChest pain with exertion, collapse/fainting, severe breathing difficulty or confusion: stop and seek urgent help. Call emergency services for severe, ongoing symptoms or collapse.
REP's coaching cue

“Fast should look relaxed. Stop while the movement is still crisp, and save enough energy to enjoy the next session.”

Rest, variety, appropriate equipment and stopping for pain reduce avoidable risk; no plan prevents every injury. Coordinate seasonal time away from tennis with the coach. [4]

A usable load rule

Example: chest press target 2 × 8-12. When both sets reach 12 clean reps for two visits, raise by the smallest available increment. If you cannot reach 8 clean reps at the new load, return to the previous one.

Choose starting loads in person, not from age or body weight. Breathe out through the harder part; avoid prolonged breath holding. Have a trainer check unfamiliar free-weight lifts.

Count actual aerobic minutes

Example week: Wednesday 30 min + Sunday 25 min + Thursday 25 min + five 15-minute brisk walks = 155 minutes. Use your actual times and count only walking brisk enough to be moderate.

At moderate effort you can talk but not sing. Short bouts count. If your current total is lower, build toward the target gradually. [1]

Health habits that support the training

Keep routine primary-care visits, blood pressure and age-appropriate preventive screening current. Discuss new exercise symptoms, known heart/lung disease or medication concerns with your clinician before harder training. Use the stop rules on page 6. Age alone does not mean every easy workout needs medical clearance.

The busy-week version
Keep two shorter gym visits: warm up, then 1-2 sets each of the first four exercises. Keep one comfortable 20-30 min run/walk and brief walking breaks. This preserves the habit; return to the full routine when the week opens up.

A missed session is not a debt. After a week off, use lighter loads and fewer sets for the first visit. After illness or injury, recovery and any clinician guidance take priority.

REP.07 / Fall + winter field rules

Change the setting.
Keep the habit.

Choose by footing, visibility, air quality and comfort, not by a commitment to train outdoors.

ConditionsPlan adjustment
Cool, dry, daylightUse the usual easy outdoor run/walk. Wear comfortable running shoes, a wicking base layer, an adjustable warm layer and wind/rain protection as needed. Start gently. [5]
Dark, wet leaves or slippery pathsChoose a well-lit, clear route with an adult accompanying the youth, or move indoors. Use reflective clothing and lights; keep hearing/attention available for traffic. No outdoor speed work on uncertain footing.
Ice, snow, severe wind chill, storms or air-quality alertsUse an indoor track, treadmill, stationary bike or elliptical. Follow local weather and air-quality advice; one temperature cutoff does not cover wind, wetness and individual tolerance.
Treadmill dayUse the same run/walk times at easy effort, initially flat. Staff must confirm youth use is permitted and teach controls. Attach the safety clip, change speed gradually and stop the belt before stepping off. No jumping onto side rails.
Bike, elliptical or pool dayReplace the running slot with 20-30 min at comfortable effort, or less if new to the activity. This maintains aerobic practice, not running-impact readiness. Repeat the last comfortable run stage when returning; step back after a longer break.
Stuck at homeTry 10-20 min easy dancing or walking indoors. Optional gentle chair squats, wall push-ups and balance practice. Rest is also a valid choice; do not invent sprint drills in a cramped room.

A simple mobility finish / 5-8 minutes

After training

Walk slowly until breathing settles. Then gently stretch the calf, front of hip and chest for 20-30 seconds per side, once or twice. Add 5 slow upper-back rotations per side.

Use a wall for calf support, a comfortable split stance for hip flexors and a doorway for the chest. A trainer can demonstrate these.

Before training

Use dynamic movement: easy walking, marching, small leg swings with support, shoulder circles and controlled practice reps.

Stretch only to mild tension, never pain; no bouncing or forced partner stretches. Flexibility work can help comfort and range but does not cancel fatigue or guarantee injury prevention.

Gear + winter recovery

Use court shoes for tennis and suitable shoes for running; check fit as feet grow. Change out of wet clothes promptly. Bring water indoors as well as outdoors. Stop and get warm for numbness or persistent shivering; confusion or poor coordination in cold conditions warrants urgent help. [5]

REP.08 / Food for growth, strength + life

Fuel the day.
Make the defaults easy.

Share balanced meals. Adjust portions to appetite and activity. Food is not something to earn with exercise.

Youth priority

Growth + steady energy

Offer regular breakfast, lunch and dinner, with snacks as needed. Avoid calorie targets, fasting, weight-loss challenges and frequent weigh-ins. A pediatrician can assess growth and whether any weight-focused treatment is appropriate. [6]

Adult priority

Strength + heart health

Include a protein food at meals, plenty of produce, fiber-rich grains and mostly unsaturated fats. Favor beans, lentils, fish, tofu, nuts and lean proteins; limit sugary drinks and frequent processed meats. [7]

Build a flexible plate

Include vegetables or fruit + a carbohydrate food + a protein food, with some healthy fat. Rice, potatoes, oats, pasta and bread can all fuel activity. Choose whole grains often. Include milk, yogurt or a suitable fortified alternative for calcium; check labels because plant drinks differ in protein and fortification.

For the growing athlete, leave room for seconds and extra carbohydrate on active days. This is a meal-building prompt, not a portion cap. For the adult, use appetite, energy and recovery to guide portions; avoid crash dieting while trying to establish training.

MomentSimple options
BreakfastOats + milk or fortified soy drink + fruit; or eggs/tofu + whole-grain toast + fruit.
School / work lunchRice or grain bowl with chicken, tofu or beans and vegetables; or a sandwich/wrap with a protein filling, fruit and yogurt.
Before activityIf the last meal was several hours ago, try a banana, toast or yogurt 30-90 min beforehand. Choose familiar foods; adjust timing for stomach comfort.
After activityA normal meal within the next couple of hours works. If dinner is delayed, use a snack such as yogurt + fruit, or milk/soy drink + toast.
Family dinnerFish, tofu, lentils or lean meat + rice/potatoes/pasta + vegetables. Frozen produce and canned beans make busy nights easier.

Drinks + supplements

Bring a bottle, start hydrated and take regular water breaks; do not force large volumes. Water is usually sufficient for these shorter sessions. Longer, sweaty tennis may need a more specific fluid/fueling plan with the coach. Avoid energy drinks and pre-workouts for the youth. Protein powders, fat burners and muscle-building supplements are not part of this plan; discuss any supplement with a clinician or sports dietitian.

Adult: if you do not drink alcohol, do not start for health; if you do, limit it. [7] Seek individual nutrition support for allergies, restricted diets, persistent fatigue, poor growth, or anxiety around food. Meal and snack combinations above are practical suggestions, not a calorie prescription.

REP.09 / Habits for school, university + work

Build a life that
makes training possible.

The transferable skills are planning, recovery, self-awareness and asking for help. Fitness does not need to become another exam.

Youth sleep
8-10 hours

Keep a steady wake time and work backward for bedtime. If wake-up is 6:30 am, being asleep around 9:30 pm gives 9 hours. Leave wind-down time before that. [8]

Adult sleep
7+ hours

Protect a reliable sleep window. Switch off devices at least 30 min before bed; avoid late caffeine. Persistent snoring, gasping or daytime sleepiness deserves a clinician conversation. [8]

The 10-minute Sunday check-in

  1. Look ahead: put tests, homework, work deadlines and tennis on one calendar.
  2. Book the anchors: agree on two family gym times and the easiest run/walk slots.
  3. Prepare: pack shoes and a bottle; choose two easy dinners and school/work snacks.
  4. Check recovery: discuss energy, enjoyment and aches without comparing bodies or performance.

For the student: independence in small steps

Let the teen choose a route, a playlist or one exercise alternative. Practice focused study blocks of about 25-45 minutes with a brief movement break. Pack the next day's bag before winding down. Keep friendships, hobbies and unstructured play in the week.

As school demands rise, practice shopping for a simple meal, cooking it, using a calendar and recognizing when to ask a teacher, coach or counselor for help. These habits also travel well to university. Sports participation is not a guarantee of academic results or admissions outcomes.

For the adult: a routine that survives work

Protect gym appointments like other commitments, stand or walk between long periods of sitting, and keep an easy lunch available. A family walk can be a useful transition out of work. Model stopping when tired and restarting after interruptions.

During exams, deadlines or a difficult week
Use shorter visits or temporarily keep just one. Drop sprints and progression; choose walking and sleep when recovery is poor. Do not double sessions afterward. Resume the last comfortable level, stepping back if the break was longer.
REP's coaching cue

“Ask: what felt better this week? Celebrate the choice to show up, the courage to ask for help, and the judgment to rest.”
